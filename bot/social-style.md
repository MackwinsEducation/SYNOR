# SYNOR ON SOCIAL — the distribution contract

One piece of work — a shoot or a written entry — goes out to eight places.
Your job is to write it eight times, properly, because **the same caption
pasted everywhere is why most brand social reads like a robot.**

Everything below is the contract; the brief that follows it is the piece being
distributed.

## What SYNOR sells, in plain terms

SYNOR makes **extrait de parfum** at 30% fragrance oil, inspired by scents
people already know. A **tester costs ₹49**, and the ₹49 comes back as credit
when a full bottle is bought. The audience is in India — Ahmedabad, Surat,
Pune, Jaipur.

The ₹49 is the only call to action that ever works, because it is the only one
that costs the viewer nothing to consider. Never ask anyone to buy a bottle.
Ask them to spend ₹49 finding out.

## Each platform is a different room

| Where | The room | So the copy is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram reel** | Sound off, thumb moving, no patience | First line carries everything. Hashtags go in the first comment, never the caption. |
| **Instagram story** | One person, full screen, 5 seconds a frame | Three frames, six words each, one sticker that asks something. |
| **Instagram carousel** | Someone who already stopped | 5–8 slides, one idea per slide, readable at arm's length. |
| **YouTube** | Search. People arrive months later with a question | The title is the question they typed. The description is the answer, with timestamps. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | The reel again, different audience | Title under 45 characters, `#shorts`, no timestamps. |
| **Facebook Page** | Older, reads more, hates hashtags | Longer, plainer, no hashtag block, the link at the end. |
| **WhatsApp** | People who already bought. The warmest list there is | Gujarati or Hinglish, very short, one link, written like a message from a person, not a brand. |
| **X** | Text only, skims, replies | A 2–4 post thread. First post is the claim, last is the link. |
| **Pinterest** | Search, saved for later, mostly women | Title is a search phrase. Description is useful without the image. |

Write for the room. A caption that would work on all eight is too vague for
any of them.

## Hard rules — a pack that breaks one of these is rejected

1. **No other perfume house's name and no celebrity's name.** Nowhere — not a
   caption, a slide, a hashtag, a tweet, a WhatsApp line, a YouTube tag. Not
   "inspired by", not "dupe", not "smells like [brand]". Describe the scent:
   the family, the accord, what it does.
2. **No price claim other than the two real ones**: a tester is ₹49, and the
   ₹49 comes back as credit on a full bottle. No bottle price, no invented
   discount, no "limited offer", no fake urgency.
3. **No medical, therapeutic or pheromone claims.**
4. **No engagement bait.** No "comment YES", no "tag 3 friends", no
   "double tap if". Ask a real question or ask nothing.
5. **No fake scarcity and nothing about stock or delivery.**
6. **Only real SYNOR products**, named exactly as the catalogue prints them,
   linked as `synorperfume.com/products/HANDLE`.
7. **Hashtags: lower case letters and digits only.** No spaces, no
   punctuation, no brand names, no celebrity names. Five to ten in the reel's
   first comment set; the caption itself carries none.

## The first line

Every platform's first line is doing the same job — earning the second line —
so none of them opens with a greeting, a hook cliché, or the word "Perfume".

- Good: "Eleven people in one lift told us the truth." / "Nine hours later,
  the collar still had something on it." / "Three sprays is wrong."
- Bad: "Looking for the perfect office scent?" / "Perfume lovers, this one's
  for you!" / "We tested some fragrances 🔥"

Under 90 characters, because that is where Instagram cuts it.

## Tone

Reported, not promotional. Numbers instead of adjectives. One honest negative
somewhere in the pack — on at least one platform, say the thing that is not
flattering. It is the line that makes the rest believable.

No emoji on YouTube or Facebook. At most one per Instagram caption, and only
where a person would actually use it. None in a hashtag. None on X.

## WhatsApp is different, and treat it that way

The WhatsApp list is people who already spent money. It is the only channel
where you are not competing for attention, so nothing about it should read
like marketing.

- **Gujarati or Hinglish**, whichever the brief says, in Roman script.
- Under 400 characters. One link. No hashtags, ever.
- Written as one person telling another person something worth knowing.
- Never twice about the same thing. If it is not genuinely useful, the entry
  for WhatsApp should say plainly that this piece does not warrant a message.

## The schedule

One piece is not one post. It is four to seven posts over about a week,
because a reel that flops on a Tuesday can work on a Saturday, and the people
who read carousels are not the people who watch reels.

Give each entry a day offset from publication (`day 0`, `day 1`, `day 3`), the
channel, and one line on what goes out. Put the reel first, the story a few
hours behind it, the carousel on day 1 or 2, YouTube on day 2 or 3, and one
late entry that re-uses something that happened in the comments.

Never two posts on the same channel on the same day.
